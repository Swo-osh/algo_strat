VIX Term Structure Mean Reversion Strategy
Writeup Checklist and Evidence Plan

Submission constraint
- Target 2-3 pages of written content plus no more than 1 page of charts and tables.
- Keep the final pitch concise: state the hypothesis, define the rules, show the evidence, and explain when the strategy fails.

1. Core concept: explain the trade in one paragraph
- State the thesis: when short-term implied volatility becomes unusually high relative to medium-term implied volatility, the shock is often temporary and can create a short-term equity rebound opportunity.
- Define the signal clearly: ratio = VIX / VIX3M.
- Explain the regimes:
	- ratio below 1.0: normal contango.
	- ratio above 1.0: backwardation.
	- ratio above the chosen entry threshold: extreme backwardation and a trade signal.
- State the trade precisely: enter long SPY on the next session after the ratio crosses the threshold; exit when the ratio falls below 1.0 or when the maximum holding period is reached.
- Make the framing explicit: this is a volatility mean-reversion trade expressed through SPY, not a prediction that equities always rise.

2. Economic rationale: explain why the edge may exist
- VIX represents expected 30-day S&P 500 volatility; VIX3M represents expected 90-day volatility.
- In ordinary conditions, near-term uncertainty is usually lower than longer-term uncertainty, producing contango.
- During a shock, demand for immediate downside protection can push VIX above VIX3M, producing backwardation.
- The hypothesis is that panic pricing can overshoot the likely persistence of the shock; as uncertainty normalizes, volatility falls and equities may rebound.
- Explain the behavioral and market-mechanics intuition: urgent hedging demand, forced deleveraging, and dealer/investor repositioning can amplify short-term moves.
- Add a limitation to the rationale: a crisis can be persistent, so backwardation is not automatically a buy signal.

4. Risk assessment and controls
- Regime persistence: backwardation can last for weeks or months during a major crisis; show the worst event and the resulting drawdown.
- Gap risk: overnight equity losses can exceed a stop or intended risk limit.
- Model risk: VIX/VIX3M may identify stress without identifying the timing of the equity rebound.
- Crowding and liquidity risk: many investors may attempt similar short-volatility trades during a reversal.
- Instrument risk: if using SVXY or VIX futures in a future version, discuss roll yield, leverage, path dependence, and potential for severe losses. Do not imply SPY and short-volatility instruments have identical exposures.
- Concentration risk: signals may cluster around a small number of crisis periods.
- Controls to describe:
	- maximum holding period;
	- position-size cap;
	- reduced sizing for very severe inversions;
	- one active trade at a time;
	- drawdown or volatility stop;
	- pause/review rule if the signal remains extreme.
- State the failure condition: if the economic shock persists rather than mean-reverts, the strategy can lose money.

6. Analytical evidence: numbers that must appear in the writeup
- Backtest window and data-cleaning decisions.
- Total number of trades and average trades per year.
- Win rate, average trade return, median trade return, best trade, and worst trade.
- Average and maximum holding period.
- Total return, annualized return, annualized volatility, Sharpe ratio, and maximum drawdown.
- Market exposure percentage and transaction-cost assumption.
- Compare against SPY buy-and-hold and SHY/cash returns over the same dates.
- Report results before and after transaction costs.
- Report development/in-sample and out-of-sample results separately. Use an out-of-sample period that was not used to choose the threshold.
- Include crisis-period results for at least 2007-2009, 2020, and 2022, or explain why a period is unavailable.
- Include parameter sensitivity for thresholds 1.00, 1.05, 1.10, and 1.20 and holding periods 10, 20, and 30 days.
- Add uncertainty: confidence intervals, bootstrap results, or a t-test against unconditional SPY forward returns.
- Discuss sample-size risk if the number of independent events is small; avoid presenting one high Sharpe ratio as proof of a durable edge.
